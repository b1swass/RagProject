Attacking with 4-3-3

When attacking, the main objective is to create width and passing options. The two wingers stay relatively wide, forcing the opposition's defensive line to cover more space. This creates gaps between defenders.

The central midfielder or defensive midfielder usually helps start the attack by receiving the ball from the centre-backs. The other midfielders can move forward to support the striker and wingers.

The fullbacks can also push forward. For example, when the left winger moves inside toward the centre, the left-back can overlap on the outside. This creates a 2-v-1 situation against the opposing fullback.
Defending with 4-3-3

When possession is lost, the team should immediately try to counter-press and win the ball back. If that isn't possible, the team drops into a compact defensive shape.

The three forwards can press the opposition's defenders and goalkeeper. The striker often tries to force the ball toward one side, while the winger closes down the opposing fullback.

Behind them, the three midfielders need to remain compact and protect the central areas. The defensive midfielder is particularly important because they can cover spaces when another midfielder or fullback moves forward.
